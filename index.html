Debmalya Mandal

Assistant Professor of Computer Science, University of Warwick

I study how AI systems should learn and decide when people, incentives and societies are part of the loop — from aligning models with human feedback to designing fair collective decisions.

I am a member of the Foundations of AI & ML group at Warwick DCS. Before Warwick, I was a postdoc at the Max Planck Institute for Software Systems with Goran Radanovic and at Columbia’s Data Science Institute with Daniel Hsu. I received my PhD in Computer Science from Harvard, advised by David C. Parkes.

Research

AI and multi-agent systems, with an emphasis on theory and modelling and applications to societal problems.

Learning from human feedback

Making RLHF and preference-based alignment hold up when feedback is corrupted, strategic, shifted or multilingual.

See: Distributionally robust RLHF · Strategyproof RLHF

All papers on this topic

RL in strategic and changing worlds

Performative reinforcement learning, principal–agent problems and multi-agent RL, where the learner’s policy reshapes its own environment.

See: Performative RL · Stochastic principal–agent problems

All papers on this topic

Robustness

Learning guarantees that survive corrupted data, poisoning attacks and distribution shift, from offline and multi-agent RL to learning from human feedback.

See: Corruption robust RLHF · Corruption-robust zero-sum Markov games

All papers on this topic

Social choice and information elicitation

Voting with limited communication, surprisingly popular voting and peer prediction — getting good collective answers from people.

See: Efficient and thrifty voting · Surprisingly popular voting

All papers on this topic

Fairness and causality

Fairness guarantees beyond the training distribution, socially fair RL, and causal effect analysis in multi-agent systems.

See: Fairness beyond the training data · Agent-specific effects
